Why it happens here

A dry climate doesn’t mean a dry house.

The Boise area gets about a foot of rain a year, so mold can seem like a problem for wetter places. Most of the mold calls in the Treasure Valley don’t start with the weather, though. They start with water the house brings in itself.

Irrigation raises the water table. From spring into fall, canals and laterals carry water across Ada and Canyon County. Groundwater near many neighborhoods rises in summer, and basements and crawlspaces that stay dry in March can get damp by July.

Cold snaps break pipes. January lows regularly drop into the teens. Supply lines in exterior walls, garages and crawlspaces freeze and split, and the leak often doesn’t show until the thaw.

Winter air holds moisture indoors. Tight newer homes and closed-up older ones trap the moisture from showers, cooking and people. It condenses on cold attic sheathing, window frames and north-facing closet walls.

Each of these needs the moisture source found and fixed, not just the visible mold cleaned off. That’s the main thing we bring.

Before you call

Three things worth doing in the next ten minutes

None of these replace a professional look, but they make that visit faster and protect your claim if you have one.

  • Stop the water if you canKnow where your main shutoff is. It’s usually in the garage, a basement utility room or a curb box near the street.
  • Take photos before anything movesWide shots of the room, close-ups of stains, and a photo of anything that failed (the hose, the fitting, the water heater label).
  • Don’t scrub or spray visible moldDry brushing and bleach can spread spores and hide the extent of the problem from whoever inspects it.

Common questions

Who will actually come to my home?

Boise Mold Experts — local, based right here in the Treasure Valley. We listen to what’s going on, come out to your home, do the assessment and give you our own estimate, so you always know who you’re working with.

Is there a cost to call?

No. Calling us is free and there’s no obligation. If there’s a charge for an inspection or the work, we’ll explain it up front, before anything starts.

What if this happens in the middle of the night?

Call us. For active water — a burst line or a flooding basement — getting help fast is the priority, and we’ll tell you straight what to expect when you call.

Is a little bathroom mold worth calling about?

If it’s a small patch on a non-porous surface that comes off and doesn’t come back, probably not. If it keeps returning, covers more than about ten square feet, or there’s a musty smell with no visible source, it’s worth a professional look.

Will my homeowner’s insurance cover it?

It depends on the cause. Sudden events like a burst pipe are often covered, while long-term seepage and neglected leaks often aren’t. Photos and a written moisture report from us help with any claim.
