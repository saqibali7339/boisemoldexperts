Room by room

Where is the mold?

Open the room that matches. Each one lists the usual cause, the warning signs and where DIY stops.

01Bathroom

The most common room for mold in any house. In the Boise area the cause is usually a bath fan that’s missing, undersized or vents into the attic instead of outside. In winter, shower moisture has nowhere to go.

  • Black spotting in grout and caulk lines that returns within weeks
  • Peeling paint or bubbling on the ceiling above the shower
  • A soft or stained baseboard next to the tub, a sign of a leaking tub drain or shower pan
Usual causePoor ventilation, failed caulk, leaking drain
DIY OKSurface spotting on tile and grout
Call whenDrywall is soft or growth returns fast
02Attic

Attic mold in the Treasure Valley usually shows up as gray-black staining on the underside of the roof sheathing, often on the north side. Warm, moist house air leaks up through can lights, bath fans and the attic hatch, then condenses on cold wood during our winter inversions.

  • Frost on nail tips in January, then drips and stains when it melts
  • Dark sheathing near bath fan ducts that end in the attic
  • Damp or matted insulation along the eaves where vents are blocked
Usual causeAir leaks, poor venting, bath fans into attic
DIY OKRerouting a bath fan duct outdoors — with proper electrical and roof-penetration safety precautions
Call whenStaining covers multiple sheets of sheathing
03Closet or exterior wall

Closets on outside walls stay colder than the rest of the room, especially behind packed clothing where air doesn’t move. Older Boise homes with little wall insulation see this most, on north- and west-facing walls.

  • Spotting low on the wall or behind stored boxes
  • Musty-smelling clothes or shoes
  • Mold that appears every winter and fades in summer
Usual causeCold wall + trapped humid air
DIY OKCleaning painted surfaces, improving air flow
Call whenIt’s inside the drywall or behind baseboards
04Kitchen, under the sink

A slow drip from a supply line, dishwasher connection or disposal gasket can soak the cabinet floor for months before anyone notices. Particleboard swells and molds quickly once it’s wet.

  • Swollen or delaminating cabinet floor
  • Musty smell when you open the cabinet
  • Staining on the wall behind the dishwasher or fridge
Usual causeSlow plumbing leaks
DIY OKFixing the leak, drying a small area
Call whenWall or subfloor is wet or stained
05Windows and sills

Condensation runs down cold glass and pools on the sill. On older aluminum-frame windows, common in 1960s–80s Boise and Nampa homes, it’s almost guaranteed in January.

  • Black spotting in corners of the frame
  • Soft or stained wood sills
  • Peeling paint below the window
Usual causeIndoor humidity on cold glass
DIY OKCleaning frames, running a dehumidifier
Call whenThe sill or wall below is soft
06Crawlspace

Many Meridian, Nampa and Kuna homes sit on crawlspaces. A torn or missing vapor barrier, a leaking line or rising irrigation-season groundwater can turn the crawlspace damp, and that air moves up into the house.

  • A musty smell strongest in the floor-level rooms
  • White fuzzy growth on joists or the underside of the subfloor
  • Standing water or wet soil under the plastic
Usual causeGroundwater, plumbing leaks, bad vapor barrier
DIY OKChecking for standing water with a flashlight
Call whenJoists show growth or water is pooling

The line between DIY and calling us

The ten-square-foot rule

Common industry guidance, including the U.S. EPA’s, says a homeowner can usually clean up an area smaller than about ten square feet, roughly a 3 ft × 3 ft patch, if the moisture problem is fixed and nobody in the home is sensitive to mold.

You can probably handle it if…

It’s small, on a hard surface, the cause is obvious and fixed, and it doesn’t come back after cleaning.

Call a professional if…

It’s larger than ten square feet, inside a wall or HVAC system, came from sewage or dirty water, keeps returning, or someone in the home has asthma or a weakened immune system.

Questions

Common questions

What’s the difference between mold removal and remediation?

In practice the terms overlap. We use “removal” for smaller, single-area jobs and “remediation” for larger work that needs full containment and clearance. See the remediation process for the full version.

Is black mold more dangerous?

“Black mold” usually refers to Stachybotrys, but many molds are dark in color, and you can’t tell the species by looking. Any significant indoor mold growth should be removed regardless of color.

Will painting over mold fix it?

No. Mold-resistant paint can help on clean, dry surfaces after the problem is fixed, but painting over active growth traps moisture and the mold continues underneath.

Does a dehumidifier help?

Indoor humidity in Boise is low most of the year, but a dehumidifier helps in basements, crawlspaces and closed-up winter homes. It manages humidity but won’t fix a leak.
