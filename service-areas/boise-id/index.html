Boise climate

Dry summers, inversion winters

Boise summers are hot and very dry, and most water problems in July and August come from plumbing or irrigation, not weather. Winter changes that. Cold air settles into the valley during inversions, attics stay below freezing for days, and indoor moisture condenses on the coldest surfaces it can find.

January and February bring the most frozen and burst pipe calls, especially in older homes with plumbing in exterior walls or crawlspaces. Spring snowmelt from the foothills and the Boise River’s high-water season add basement seepage on lower lots.

Questions

Common questions

Do you cover all of Boise?

We help homeowners in every Boise ZIP code, including the North End, Bench, Southeast, West Boise, the Foothills, Harris Ranch and Garden City nearby.

Are older North End homes more prone to mold?

They’re more prone to certain moisture sources, mainly basement seepage through older foundations and leaks from aging plumbing. Plaster walls also behave differently from drywall during remediation.

When do most Boise water damage calls happen?

Frozen and burst pipes peak in January and February. Basement seepage rises in spring and during irrigation season. Plumbing and appliance failures happen year-round.
