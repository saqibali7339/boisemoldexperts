Know your walls

Three wall types common in Boise homes

Knock on the wall. Plaster sounds solid and feels cold and hard, while drywall sounds hollow between studs. A light switch plate removed (power off) shows the layers in cross-section.

Plaster on wood lath Common before ~1940

Thick, dense and slow to show water. Mold often grows on the wood lath and framing behind it before any stain shows on the face. Remediation means cutting out plaster sections, which is dusty and heavy, so tight containment matters even more. We often suggest asbestos and lead testing first in homes this age.

Rock lath or early gypsum board Roughly 1940s–1960s

Gypsum panels with a plaster skim coat, common on the Bench and in post-war ranches. They absorb water faster than full plaster but slower than modern drywall. Joint compound and textured finishes from this era may contain asbestos, so testing before demolition is standard practice.

Modern drywall 1970s–today

Paper-faced gypsum that wicks water quickly and feeds mold on its paper backing. Easy to cut and replace, so remediation usually means removing the affected section plus a margin, with the edges sealed off inside containment.

Older Boise homes

Test before you tear out

The U.S. EPA notes that materials in homes built before the 1980s may contain asbestos, and that lead paint is common in homes built before 1978. Disturbing them during mold removal can create a bigger health problem than the mold.

For a Boise home in that age range, a responsible remediation plan usually includes sampling suspect materials, like plaster, joint compound, popcorn ceilings and old flooring, before cutting into them. If they test positive, abatement is handled under separate rules before mold work continues.

That can add a few days to the schedule. It’s also the step most likely to be skipped by someone who isn’t qualified, so ask about it.

Where it shows up

Common Boise remediation spots

North-wall bedrooms

Older homes with little or no wall insulation get condensation on cold north and west walls during winter inversions.

Original bathrooms

Pre-1970 bathrooms often have no exhaust fan, only a window that stays shut all winter.

Partial basements

Stone and early concrete foundations in the North End let groundwater through, especially in spring.

Questions

Common questions

Can mold grow behind plaster walls?

Yes. Plaster itself resists mold better than paper-faced drywall, but the wood lath and framing behind it can grow mold when they stay wet. Signs include a musty smell, bubbling paint or soft spots.

Will remediation damage my original plaster?

Affected sections usually have to be removed. Some crews can cut cleanly enough to patch with plaster or a plaster-matching finish, so ask how they handle restoration in historic homes.

Does Boise require permits for mold remediation?

Mold remediation itself usually doesn’t need a permit, but related plumbing, electrical or structural repairs may. We can tell you what applies.
