Deal timeline

Where mold inspection fits in a Boise purchase

Timelines are set by your contract, so check yours with your agent. These are typical windows.

Day 0Offer accepted

Note your inspection deadline

Many Idaho purchase contracts give buyers a set number of days for inspections. That window is your chance to investigate and negotiate.

Days 1–5Home inspection

General home inspection

The inspector flags moisture, stains, odors and ventilation issues. If anything points to mold, book a mold inspection right away instead of waiting for the final report.

Days 3–8Mold inspection

Targeted mold & moisture inspection

Moisture mapping and a visual inspection of flagged areas, with sampling if useful. Lab results usually take a few business days.

Before deadlineNegotiate

Request repairs, credit or price change

A written report with photos and a remediation scope gives you something specific to negotiate with.

Before closingVerify

Confirm the work was done

If the seller remediates, ask for the contractor’s documentation and consider independent clearance testing.

For buyers

Ask for an inspection when…

  • The home inspection notes staining, moisture or odor
  • The basement is finished in an older home
  • The house sat vacant, especially through a Boise winter
  • Fresh paint covers a single wall or ceiling patch
  • There’s a history of water damage on the disclosure
For sellers

Consider a pre-listing inspection if…

  • You’ve had a past leak or flood
  • The basement has ever been damp
  • You want to avoid surprises in negotiation
  • You’ve already remediated and want documentation

Idaho’s seller disclosure form asks about known conditions, so documentation helps.

Questions

Common questions

How quickly can I get a mold inspection in Boise?

It depends on the inspector’s schedule. Mention your contingency date when you call so it can be prioritized.

Do I need air sampling to negotiate?

Not always. A moisture and visual inspection with photos and a scope estimate is often enough. Air sampling helps when there’s a smell or symptoms without visible growth.

Should I trust the seller’s mold report?

It’s useful information, but many buyers get their own inspection, especially if the seller’s remediation company also did the testing.
