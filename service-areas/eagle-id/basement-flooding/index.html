First hour

The first hour: what to do, in order

  1. Make it safe. Keep everyone out of standing water near electrical outlets, extension cords or the furnace. Don't use a wet-dry vacuum or any plugged-in tool while standing in water.

  2. Stop the source if you can. Inside-the-house sources can be shut off at the valve. Outside sources can't, so move on to the next step.

  3. Photograph the water line. Take wide shots of each room, close-ups of the waterline on walls, and a photo of anything that failed. Do this before the water drops.

  4. Move what you can, from a dry spot. Lift furniture onto blocks or foil, pull electronics, boxes and rugs out of the water, and keep anything you can't replace out of the basement.

  5. Call for extraction. Every hour water stays in carpet pad, drywall and framing makes the job bigger. Flooded basement cleanup in Eagle starts with getting the water out fast, so call while you are still collecting photos.

The crew

What a drying crew does once they arrive

Shop vacs and towels can't pull water out of carpet pad or the bottom of a wall. Basement water damage repair in Eagle generally follows this order. The details change with the size of the basement and the source of the water.

Pump and extract

Submersible pumps handle standing water, then extraction tools pull water out of carpet and pad.

Check the water type

Water from a supply line is handled differently from water that has moved through soil or come from a drain. Industry practice (IICRC S500) sorts water into categories, and the category decides what can be dried and what has to be removed.

Cut out what can't be saved

Saturated carpet pad and the bottom section of wet drywall are removed so the framing behind them can dry. This is often called a flood cut.

Dry the structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers run while moisture readings are tracked until materials reach normal dry levels.

Check heating equipment before restarting

A furnace or water heater that was under water should be checked by a qualified technician before it is turned on.

Drying beats cleaning. EPA mold guidance says to dry water-damaged materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. For the full hour-by-hour picture, see the water damage timeline.

Insurance

Will insurance cover a basement flood?

It depends on where the water came from.

  • Sudden inside failures such as a burst pipe or failed water heater are often covered by a standard homeowner policy.
  • Water that comes in from outside through the ground, a window well or a wall is often excluded and may need separate flood coverage. Flood policies are sold separately from homeowner policies. The federal route is FEMA's National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP, via floodsmart.gov); private flood insurers (such as Neptune and Chubb) also sell separate flood policies.
  • Gradual seepage and neglected leaks are often excluded.

Read your policy, photograph everything, and call your insurer early. This is general information and not a coverage opinion.

Questions

Common questions

Can I pump the water out myself right away?

For a small amount of clean water from an inside source, possibly. If the ground outside is saturated, emptying a basement all at once can put pressure on the walls, so ask a professional before pumping out a deep flood. (FEMA's after-a-flood guidance: pump out about one-third of the water per day — the walls may collapse and the floor buckle if the basement is emptied while the surrounding ground is still waterlogged.) Never use plugged-in equipment while standing in water.

How long until mold can start after a basement floods?

Mold can start within 24 to 48 hours on wet materials that haven't been dried, according to EPA guidance. That is why extraction and drying come before any cleaning. If mold has already appeared, see basement mold removal in Eagle.

What gets thrown away and what can be saved?

Carpet pad and saturated drywall at the bottom of the walls usually go. Framing, concrete and some flooring can often be dried and kept. Anything that touched sewage or contaminated water is treated more strictly. A moisture reading is the only reliable way to decide.

Will the basement flood again next spring?

It can, if the cause isn't fixed. Pulling the water out and drying the structure repairs the damage but not the entry point. In Eagle that often means sump pump capacity near the river, or grading and window wells on a slope.
