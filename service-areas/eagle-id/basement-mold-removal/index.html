Wall by wall

How each wall of a walkout basement behaves

Uphill wall

Fully buried

Highest risk. Runoff from the slope, irrigation from uphill yards and snowmelt all collect here. Watch for damp carpet edges, stained baseboards and mold low on finished walls.

Side walls

Stepped down the slope

Risk is highest at the uphill end and drops toward the walkout. Moisture often shows as a diagonal pattern that follows the grade line outside.

Floor slab

Under everything

Hydrostatic pressure from a high water table, more common near the river corridor than in the foothills, can push water up through cracks and the floor-wall joint.

Walkout wall

Exposed, above grade

Lowest groundwater risk, but door thresholds, window flashing and patio drainage can still let water in.

Two kinds of Eagle basement

Foothills and river corridor are different problems

In the foothills, the issue is usually water moving across and through the soil from above. Clay-heavy soils in parts of the foothills hold water against the foundation. The fix often involves surface grading, a curtain drain on the uphill side and checking that sprinklers aren’t soaking the slope.

Along the Boise River corridor, the issue is usually water rising from below as the water table climbs in spring and during irrigation. Sump pumps, interior perimeter drains and dehumidification matter more there.

In both cases, mold removal follows the same steps: containment, removing the lower section of affected drywall and insulation, cleaning and drying the framing. Rebuilding with moisture-tolerant materials keeps it from coming back.

Questions

Common questions

Why is only one side of my basement damp?

In walkout and hillside basements, the uphill wall carries most of the soil and water load. Damp on that wall alone strongly suggests surface or subsurface water from upslope.

Will a dehumidifier solve basement mold?

It helps control humidity, but it won’t stop water coming through a wall. The water entry needs to be fixed before remediation will last.

Are newer Eagle homes waterproofed?

Most have foundation damp-proofing and perimeter drains, but these can be overwhelmed by landscaping changes, clogged drains or irrigation that soaks the slope.
