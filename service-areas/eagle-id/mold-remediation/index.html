Bigger homes

More house, more places for mold to hide

A bigger house has more water points, and every one of them is a place mold can start unseen: an extra bathroom, a laundry room, a wet bar, a long plumbing run. Rooms that do not get used every day are the quietest risk, a guest bath, a bonus room, a storage room, because a slow leak there can run for weeks before anyone notices.

That is why the visible patch is rarely the whole job. Remediation in a large home starts with an on-site assessment of every room the water could have reached, and testing where growth is suspected but not visible. See mold testing in Eagle for how hidden growth is found.

Three settings

Where Eagle mold shows up

Eagle covers three kinds of ground, and moisture problems show up differently in each. The setting shapes where we look first.

  • River corridor. South Eagle near the Boise River and its channels, including the Eagle Island area: shallow groundwater and sandy, gravelly soils. Watch for basement and crawlspace dampness in spring high water and through irrigation season.
  • Ranch and acreage lots. North of State Street toward Floating Feather and beyond: flatter land, often on wells and septic, with irrigation ditches. Watch for irrigation runoff toward the house and plumbing failures on well-pressure systems.
  • Foothills. Hillside neighborhoods north and northeast of town: sloped lots, clay soils in places, walkout basements. Watch for water pushing against the uphill foundation wall, roof leaks on complex rooflines, and hidden plumbing leaks in large floor plans.

For the full picture of the three settings, see the Eagle page.

The job

How the job is sequenced in a large home

Once the affected rooms are mapped, the job runs zone by zone. Each zone is sealed off so spores are not tracked through the rest of the house, affected material is removed, and the zone is dried and verified before anything is rebuilt. Working in zones keeps the rest of the house usable where containment allows.

Skipping the scoping step is how a job gets done twice: a room that was never opened or checked keeps feeding spores back into rooms that were already cleaned. For the full process, see the 7-step remediation process.

Questions

Common questions

What makes Eagle homes different for mold?

Size, and three kinds of ground. Eagle homes are often larger than the valley average, with more bathrooms, second-floor laundry rooms, wet bars, and long plumbing runs, so there are more places for a slow leak to hide. Add river-corridor groundwater, acreage irrigation, and foothill slopes, and the first step is always finding every affected room.

Why does remediation cost more in a bigger house?

More area to contain, more material to remove, and longer drying. A small contained job and a multi-room job are different scopes. An on-site assessment sizes the work first, and pricing follows the rooms affected.

Can we stay in the house during the work?

Often yes, for rooms outside containment. Affected rooms are sealed off so the rest of the house stays usable, but children and pets should stay out of the work zones. If the affected area is large, ask us for a recommendation.

Do you test before and after remediation?

Testing finds growth that is suspected but not visible, and verifies drying before rebuild. It is especially useful in a large home, where an unchecked room can undo finished work.
