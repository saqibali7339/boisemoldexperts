Scope checklist

What a thorough Eagle inspection should include

Bring this list when you schedule. Ask which items are included and which cost extra.

  • Every attic spaceComplex rooflines often mean more than one attic, plus spaces over bonus rooms and garages.
  • Each HVAC systemMultiple furnaces or air handlers, with condensate lines and drain pans for each.
  • Every bathroom & wet barIncluding rarely used guest baths, where slow leaks go unnoticed.
  • Crawlspaces and basementIncluding walkout uphill walls.
  • Garage and shop spacesEspecially with plumbing or living space above.
  • Guest house or casitaSeparate structures often have separate problems.
  • Moisture readings at all wet wallsNot just where staining is visible.
  • Air samples per zone, if samplingOne indoor sample doesn’t represent a large multi-level home.

Special cases

Vacant, seasonal and new-build homes

Vacant or seasonal homes. Eagle has its share of second homes and houses that sat empty while listed. A home with the water on and the heat low through a winter, or closed up through a hot summer, is worth a closer look.

New construction. Framing that got rained or snowed on before it was dried in can carry mold into the walls. Ask whether the inspector can take moisture readings on framing during a pre-drywall walkthrough.

Homes on wells. Well water and pressure systems add a mechanical room and sometimes a separate pump house to the scope.

Questions

Common questions

Does a bigger home cost more to inspect?

Typically, yes. Inspection time and the number of samples increase with size and complexity. Agreeing on the scope up front avoids surprises.

How many air samples does a large home need?

It depends on the layout. Many inspectors take one sample per HVAC zone or per floor, plus an outdoor baseline.

Can an inspection be done before drywall in a new build?

Yes. A pre-drywall moisture check of the framing can catch construction moisture before it’s sealed in.
