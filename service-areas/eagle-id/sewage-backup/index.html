The cleanup

What sewage cleanup involves

  • Sewage is Category 3 water in ANSI/IICRC S500 guidance: grossly contaminated, the most serious category. It is handled differently from clean water.
  • CDC guidance: keep children and pets out of the affected area until cleanup is complete, and wear rubber boots, gloves and goggles during cleanup.
  • Porous materials that soaked up sewage, such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, are removed and discarded, not cleaned.
  • Hard surfaces and framing that can be kept are cleaned and disinfected, then air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings are back to normal.
  • Insurance: sewer backups are usually not covered by a standard homeowner policy. The Insurance Information Institute notes backup coverage has to be added as an endorsement, at roughly $40 to $50 a year.

Questions

Common questions

Why did only my basement bathroom back up?

In many homes with a basement bathroom, waste is pumped up to the main line by an ejector pump. If that pump fails or its discharge is blocked, only the basement fixtures back up while the rest of the house works. A plumber can confirm it.

Can I keep using the upstairs bathrooms?

Not until the cause is found. Every flush and every load of laundry sends more water into a line that is already blocked, and it can push more waste out below.

Is it safe to stay in the house?

Often yes, if the affected area is separated from the rest of the home. Keep children and pets out, don’t walk through it and then through clean rooms, and wash your hands well. If a large area is affected or anyone is unwell, ask a professional first.

Can I clean it up myself?

A small spill from a single fixture can sometimes be handled with proper protection, but porous materials that soaked it up usually can’t be cleaned. Anything larger than a small area is better left to a crew.
