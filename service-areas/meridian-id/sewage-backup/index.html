House to main

Follow the line, from your drains to the street

Every home’s line is a little different. This is the typical layout for a Meridian home on city sewer.

1 · Fixtures & branch drains Your responsibility

Sinks, tubs, toilets. If only one fixture is backing up, the clog is likely in that branch. That’s a plumber’s job, and usually not a sewage emergency.

2 · Main building drain Your responsibility

Where all the branches join under the house or slab. A clog here backs up the lowest drains first, often a ground-floor shower or tub.

3 · Cleanout Access point

A capped pipe, usually outside near the foundation. If it’s full of water when opened, the blockage is downstream, toward the street.

4 · Service lateral Typically your responsibility

The pipe from your house to the city main. Roots, grease, wipes and settled pipe are the usual causes. Many cities make homeowners responsible for this whole run, so check the City of Meridian’s rules for your address.

5 · City main City responsibility

The public sewer under the street. If several neighbors have backups at once, call the City of Meridian’s public works or wastewater line right away.

Inside your home

Meanwhile, the cleanup can’t wait

Whoever clears the line, sewage inside your home is contaminated water. Carpet, pad and drywall bottoms it touched usually have to be removed, and everything else gets disinfected and dried.

  • Keep people and pets outEspecially kids and anyone with a weakened immune system.
  • Don’t use a shop vacIt spreads contaminants into the air.
  • Photograph before cleanupInsurance often requires it.
  • Check your policy for sewer backup coverageIt’s often a separate endorsement.
Questions

Common questions

How do I know if it’s the city main?

Signs include several neighbors with backups at the same time, or a backup after heavy use across the neighborhood. Call the City of Meridian’s public works line to report it.

Will the city pay for my cleanup?

Only in specific cases, usually when the city main caused the backup. Even then, claims processes vary. Document everything and ask the city about their claims procedure.

Can I prevent future backups?

A backwater valve can help keep a city main backup out of your home. Regular camera inspection of older laterals catches root intrusion early.
