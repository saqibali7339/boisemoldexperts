Basement damp only in summer? In Nampa, that’s the irrigation calendar at work.

Nampa sits in a web of canals, laterals and drain ditches that carry water across Canyon County from spring to fall. When the canals run, groundwater rises, and so does moisture in basements and crawlspaces.

Irrigation calendar

When moisture risk rises in Nampa homes

Exact dates vary by irrigation district and water year. Most canals in the area deliver water from roughly mid-April to mid-October, and groundwater lags behind by a few weeks.

Why timing matters

Diagnose in summer, repair in fall

If your basement or crawlspace only gets damp from June to September, the best time to have it looked at is while it’s wet. Readings taken in December may show nothing, even if the problem returns every year.

Once the cause is confirmed, many Nampa homeowners schedule remediation and drainage work for late fall, after the canals shut off and groundwater drops. That gives materials the best chance to dry fully before rebuild.

Not every Nampa water problem is groundwater. Older homes near downtown and the north side have aging plumbing, and many still use evaporative (swamp) coolers, which can be a moisture source of their own.

Questions

Common questions

Why is my Nampa basement wet only in summer?

Seasonal irrigation raises groundwater in many parts of Canyon County. Water moves through soil toward lower ground, and basements and crawlspaces can sit below that seasonal water table.

Can I get the irrigation district to fix it?

Irrigation districts manage their canals and laterals, not groundwater on private property. If a canal or ditch is visibly leaking or overflowing onto your lot, contact the district. Otherwise, the fix is usually drainage on your side.

Do you serve rural Nampa too?

Yes. Calls from Nampa and the surrounding Canyon County areas, including properties outside city limits, are covered — we work the area.
