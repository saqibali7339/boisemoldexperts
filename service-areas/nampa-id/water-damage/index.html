Irrigation flood response

What to do, in order

  1. Find where it’s coming from

    Your own pressurized irrigation line? Shut the irrigation valve, usually in a box near the street or at the pump. A ditch, lateral or a neighbor’s flood irrigation? Note where it’s coming from and call the irrigation district or the ditch rider.

    First 15 minutes

  2. Divert at the house

    Sandbags, a trench or even plastic sheeting can steer surface water away from window wells and the garage door. Clear window well drains if you can reach them safely.

    First hour

  3. Check the crawlspace or basement

    Open the crawlspace hatch with a flashlight. If water is near ductwork, the furnace or wiring, shut off power to those circuits from a dry spot. Don’t go in if you’re unsure.

    First hour

  4. Document it

    Photograph the water path from the source to your home, and the water level inside. That matters if another property’s water caused it.

    Before cleanup

  5. Get pumping and extraction started

    Standing water in a crawlspace needs to be pumped out, and the vapor barrier is usually replaced. Irrigation water is treated as at least Category 2 (gray water) because of what it picks up.

    Same day

  6. Dry and watch for mold

    Crawlspaces in Nampa’s summer are warm and humid when wet, so mold can start in a couple of days without dehumidification.

    Days 1–5

Nampa specifics

Why irrigation flooding is a Canyon County thing

Nampa is surrounded by working farmland, and many neighborhoods were built right up against canals, laterals and drain ditches. Some older properties and acreage still use flood (surface) irrigation, sending water across the lot on a set schedule.

Problems usually come from a turnout left open, a check gate that fails, debris blocking a lateral, or a neighbor’s flood water crossing a property line. Newer subdivisions on pressurized irrigation can have a broken line flood a yard overnight.

When another property’s water damages your home, documentation and prompt notice matter. Your insurer or an attorney can advise on responsibility.

Questions

Common questions

Is irrigation water dangerous to clean up?

It’s not sewage, but it often carries soil, fertilizer, animal waste and debris. Restoration crews usually treat it as gray water, which means more cleaning and sometimes removing porous materials.

Does insurance cover irrigation flooding?

Surface water flooding is often excluded from standard homeowner’s policies. If another party caused it, their liability may come into play. Check with your insurer.

Who do I call about a leaking canal?

Contact the irrigation district or canal company that operates it. Many have after-hours numbers during irrigation season.
